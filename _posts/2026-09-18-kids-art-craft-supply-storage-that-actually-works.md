Craft supplies multiply fast — markers dry out loose in a drawer, paper gets crumpled under other stuff, and a "quick coloring project" turns into fifteen minutes of digging for the right marker before anyone draws a single line. The gap isn't a lack of storage, it's storage that isn't actually set up for how a kid grabs supplies mid-project.

These four products cover the real friction points: finding the supply you want without dumping a bin, moving the whole craft station to wherever the table is, dispensing paper without waste, and giving art projects a dedicated surface instead of taking over the kitchen table.

Craft time doesn't always happen at the same table, and hauling supplies back and forth in your arms means something gets dropped. ## How to choose kids art and craft supply storage that actually works

Visibility beats capacity — a rotating caddy that shows every marker at once gets used more than a deep bin that hides half its contents at the bottom. Give the system wheels or portability if craft time doesn't happen in one fixed spot in your house, since supplies that have to be carried by hand tend to get left wherever the project ended. And keep sharp tools like paper cutters positioned for supervised use rather than fully independent access, especially for younger kids.

## FAQ

**What's the best way to keep kids' markers from drying out?**
Storage isn't usually the real problem — it's caps left off during projects. A rotating caddy that makes putting markers back easy (rather than tossing them loose in a bin) meaningfully cuts down on lost caps and dried-out markers.

**How do I organize craft supplies for more than one kid sharing them?**
A rolling cart with labeled drawers works well for multiple kids, since it can be wheeled to whichever kid is working and the drawer categories keep supplies from turning into one big mixed pile.

**Is a wall-mounted paper cutter safe for kids to use?**
Only with supervision for younger kids — the blade is genuinely sharp. Mount it at a height where a kid can pull and position the paper themselves, but have an adult handle the actual cutting motion until they're old enough.
