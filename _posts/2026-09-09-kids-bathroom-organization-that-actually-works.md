## How to choose kids bathroom organization that actually works

Design around what a kid can reach and operate alone, not what looks organized to an adult standing at full height — a step stool, a low hook, and a scoop that requires no lid or latch are what actually turn "go brush your teeth" into something a child can do without help. Prioritize anything that dries out completely between uses (mesh over solid plastic, drainage holes over flat bins) since kids' bathroom items are wet far more often than adult ones. And build in a system for shared items — assigned toothbrush slots, one hook per kid — since shared-without-structure is where kids bathrooms get messy fastest.

## FAQ

**What height should bathroom hooks be for a young child to use them independently?**
Roughly shoulder height for the child using them, well below the standard 60" adult towel-bar height — a hook that requires reaching up doesn't get used consistently by younger kids.

**How do I keep bath toys from growing mold in the tub?**
Store them in a quick-drying mesh organizer that hangs and drains after every bath rather than leaving them in a pile at the bottom of the tub — standing water sitting inside toys is what actually causes the mold, not the toys themselves.

**What's the best way to organize toothbrushes for multiple kids sharing one bathroom?**
A multi-slot holder with a separate compartment per child, ideally with each slot assigned and labeled — it prevents brushes from touching each other and ends the daily confusion over whose brush is whose.
