Adding a second or third screen solves a productivity problem and creates a desk-space one — more monitors means more stands, more cables, and more chances for screens to end up at mismatched heights that leave your neck sore by the afternoon.

These four products cover the real multi-screen situations: two monitors on arms, three monitors on a desk, one monitor added to a laptop, and the docking hardware that ties it all together with a single cable.

## How to choose a monitor setup that actually works

Match the hardware to how many screens you're actually adding, not the maximum you could theoretically fit — a single arm is the right call for a laptop-plus-one setup, and a triple riser is overkill until you're genuinely running three displays daily. Get the heights matched before worrying about cables; mismatched monitor height causes more daily discomfort than cable clutter does. And if the setup involves a laptop that gets unplugged and moved regularly, a docking station is worth the cost just for how much faster it makes disconnecting and reconnecting.

## FAQ

**Do I need a monitor arm if I'm only adding one external screen to a laptop?**
Yes, generally — a single arm gets that one monitor to the correct eye height, which matters more with a laptop setup since the laptop's built-in screen is already too low to use as your primary reference point.

**What's the minimum desk width for a triple monitor setup?**
Around 50" is the practical minimum for three monitors side by side without cramming — measure your desk before buying a triple stand, since the stand itself won't shrink the physical footprint of three screens.

**Is a USB-C docking station worth it for a two-monitor setup?**
It's most worth it if the laptop moves daily; for a laptop that stays permanently docked, direct cable connections work fine and a hub isn't strictly necessary.
