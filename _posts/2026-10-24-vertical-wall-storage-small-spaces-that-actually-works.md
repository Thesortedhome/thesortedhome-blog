Floor space in a small home runs out fast, but wall space almost never does — most walls above waist height sit completely empty in every room of a small apartment. Vertical wall storage is the fix, and it works in almost any room, not just one.

These four products all turn blank wall space into real, functional storage, from a fully modular pegboard system down to a simple hanging basket.

## 1. A modular pegboard system for storage that rearranges as needs change

## 2. Hanging wall baskets for no-drill vertical storage

## How to choose vertical wall storage that actually works

Start by auditing which walls in your home are currently blank above waist height — that's unused storage, full stop, whether or not it feels that way. Match the mounting method to your lease: pegboards and cabinets typically need drilling, while adhesive-hook baskets and leaning shelves don't, so check what your rental actually allows before committing to a system. And mix open and closed storage across the walls you use — open baskets and pegboards for frequently grabbed items, closed cabinets for anything you'd rather not see every day.

## FAQ

**What's the best vertical wall storage option for a rental that doesn't allow drilling?**
A leaning ladder shelf or adhesive-hook wall baskets — both add real wall-adjacent storage without a single hole, and both come down cleanly at move-out.

**Is pegboard or a wall-mounted cabinet better for a small space?**
It depends on what's being stored — pegboard is better for frequently accessed items that benefit from being visible and rearrangeable, while a cabinet with doors is better for anything you want out of sight, like cleaning supplies or overflow toiletries.

**How much weight can adhesive wall hooks actually hold?**
It varies by product, but most rated adhesive hooks hold several pounds each — check the specific weight rating before hanging anything heavy, and use screw-mounted hooks instead for genuinely heavy items like cast iron cookware or tools.
