Paper clutter builds up faster than almost anything else in a household because it arrives constantly — bills, statements, school forms, receipts — and most of it needs to be dealt with on a different timeline than "right now." Without a real system, that mismatch turns into stacks on the counter, and stacks on the counter turn into missed due dates.

These three products cover the three real timeframes paper needs to be sorted by: this month's bills, today's incoming mail, and anything that needs to be kept for the long haul.

## How to choose paper filing that actually works

Match each product to how often you actually need to touch that category of paper — daily mail needs a fast, visible spot; monthly bills need a system with categories; annual paperwork needs secure, out-of-the-way storage. Resist the urge to consolidate everything into one bin, since mixing timeframes is exactly what causes important bills to get buried under paperwork you only need once a year. And build in a regular clearing habit, even a five-minute weekly pass through the mail sorter — the products only work if something eventually empties them.

## FAQ

**How long should I actually keep paper bills and statements?**
Most financial guidance recommends 1 year for basic bills and utility statements, and up to 7 years for tax-related documents — anything beyond that timeframe is usually safe to shred.

**What's the fastest way to stop mail from piling up on the kitchen counter?**
Put a sorter directly where mail enters the house and open it the same day it arrives — the habit matters more than the product, but a visible sorter makes the habit easier to keep.

**Should I switch to a fully digital filing system instead of physical folders?**
A hybrid approach works best for most households — scan documents you need occasionally (tax records, warranties) and keep frequently-referenced paper (current bills) in a physical system you can grab quickly.
