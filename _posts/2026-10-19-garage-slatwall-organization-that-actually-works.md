Individual hooks and a pegboard solve garage wall storage in a limited way, but they're fixed once installed — moving a pegboard hook means patching and repainting. Slatwall panels solve that differently: the grooved panel system lets hooks, baskets, and shelves click in and reposition anywhere along the panel, with no new holes needed every time your storage needs change.

These three products cover a full slatwall build from the ground up — the panels themselves, a complete accessory kit to get an entire wall functional at once, and extra hooks for filling in gaps once you know what's actually getting mounted where.

## How to choose a garage slatwall system that actually works

Start with panel coverage sized to your actual wall, not the whole garage at once — a single 4x8 section is enough to test whether slatwall works better than your current setup before committing to a full-wall build. Buy a general accessory kit before specialty items; most slatwall failures come from over-ordering one hook type early and then discovering the wall actually needed different sizes for different tools. And remember the entire advantage of slatwall over pegboard is repositioning without new holes — resist the urge to treat the initial layout as permanent, since the format is designed to be rearranged as your storage needs change.

## FAQ

**Is slatwall better than pegboard for a garage?**
For heavier items and long-term flexibility, generally yes — slatwall accessories lock in more securely and hold more weight than pegboard hooks, and repositioning items doesn't leave visible holes the way moving a pegboard hook does.

**How much weight can a garage slatwall panel actually hold?**
It varies by accessory more than by the panel itself — most PVC slatwall systems are rated well beyond standard drywall, but the specific hook or bracket you're using determines the real weight limit for that spot, so check individual accessory ratings for anything heavy.

**Do I need a full accessory kit, or can I just buy hooks?**
A starter kit is usually worth it for a first-time install, since it reveals which accessory types you actually need. Once the wall is in regular use, buying extra hooks or a specific accessory type individually is more efficient than a second full kit.
