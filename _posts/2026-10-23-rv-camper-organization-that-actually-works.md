RV and camper organization has a constraint most small-space advice ignores completely: everything has to survive actual motion. A bin that works perfectly in a stationary apartment can dump its contents across the floor the first time the rig takes a turn, which makes generic small-space storage only half the solution.

These four products are chosen specifically for the RV problem — storage that stays put, cabinets that don't rattle open, and bins that fold flat when they're not needed for a trip.

## 1. Collapsible storage bins that fold flat between trips

RV storage bays are usually only full when the rig is loaded for a trip — the rest of the time, rigid bins just take up space they don't need to. A [3-pack of 62L collapsible storage bins](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DF7X4HZ5?tag=thesortedho09-smallspace-20) holds up to 100 lbs each fully loaded but folds down to under 3 inches thick when empty — rated 4.6 stars across 5,100+ reviews.

**Where it works best:** exterior storage bays and truck beds for bulky gear, folded flat and tucked away during the off-season or between trips when they're not holding anything.

## 2. Non-slip liner to stop cabinet contents from shifting while driving

Dishes, canned goods, and toiletries all slide the moment an RV brakes or turns, which is the single most common source of a messy camper cabinet. A [non-slip cabinet and shelf liner](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0823SZLCS?tag=thesortedho09-smallspace-20), sold in a 12"x20ft roll, grips shelves without adhesive and can be cut to fit any cabinet or drawer — rated 4.6 stars across 8,500+ reviews.

**Tip:** line every cabinet and drawer that holds anything breakable before the first trip, not just the ones that seem obviously at risk — even light contents shift more than expected on rough roads.

## 3. Tension rods to keep cabinet doors and fridge contents from sliding

Beyond liner on the shelves themselves, cabinet doors and refrigerator shelves need something actively holding contents in place during transit. A [6-pack of adjustable spring tension rods](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07P5R31FG?tag=thesortedho09-smallspace-20) wedges across a cabinet opening or fridge shelf to keep items from sliding forward — rated 4.3 stars across 6,500+ reviews.

**Where it works best:** across the front of open cabinet shelves, inside the fridge to keep bottles upright, or across a pantry shelf holding anything that isn't already boxed in.

Most RV kitchens don't have a dedicated pantry, just a couple of small cabinets. **Tip:** load heavier items on the lower tiers and lighter items up top — over-the-door organizers hang from the top, so a bottom-heavy load keeps it from swinging or straining the door hinges while driving.

## How to choose RV and camper storage that actually works

Everything in an RV needs to answer one question generic home storage doesn't: what happens to this while the vehicle is moving? Favor bins with secure lids or straps over open bins, liner or rods for anything that can slide on a shelf, and collapsible storage over rigid storage wherever the space isn't used year-round. And weigh what you're adding — RVs and campers have real payload limits, so storage solutions that are lightweight when empty matter more here than in a stationary home.

## FAQ

**How do I keep RV cabinets from opening while driving?**
Childproof cabinet latches paired with tension rods across shelf contents solve most of this — the latches keep doors shut, and the rods stop what's inside from shifting hard enough to pop them open.

**What's the best way to organize an RV kitchen with no pantry?**
An over-the-door organizer is usually the fastest fix, since it adds shelf-equivalent storage using a door that would otherwise go unused, without requiring any cabinet modification.

**Does RV storage need to be different from regular home organization products?**
For anything holding loose contents, yes — regular home bins and shelves aren't designed for the vibration and turns of actual driving, so non-slip liner, tension rods, and secured bins matter more in an RV than they would in a stationary home.
