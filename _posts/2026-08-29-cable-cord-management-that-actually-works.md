A single cable tray handles the cords hanging under the desk, but that's only one piece of the problem. Individual cords still snake across the desktop, the power strip itself is usually just sitting loose on the floor, and every setup has a few feet of extra slack that has to go somewhere once everything's plugged in.

## How to choose cable management that actually works

Treat this as four separate problems, not one — the cord bundle traveling to the wall, the individual cords that don't travel with the pack, the power strip itself, and the leftover slack all need their own fix, and a single cable tray usually only solves the first. Prioritize whichever one is most visible in your specific setup; a strip sitting loose on the floor reads as messier than a tidy bundle of cords, even if the total cord count is the same. And favor closed, contained solutions (sleeves, clips, boxes) over open trays wherever cords are visible from a seated position — open trays still show a tangle from the wrong angle.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between a cable sleeve and a cable management box?**
A sleeve wraps and bundles cords along a run (desk to wall, for example), while a box hides a contained pile of slack or a power strip in one spot — most setups need both, not one or the other.

**How do I stop my power strip from just sitting on the floor?**
A clamp-mount power strip solves this directly by attaching to the desk's edge instead of resting on the floor — it's a different product from a cable tray, which organizes cords but doesn't relocate the strip.

**Do adhesive cable clips actually hold up long-term, or do they fall off?**
Quality 3M-backed clips hold well on clean, dry surfaces for years, but they do lose grip on textured or oily desk finishes — wipe the mounting spot with rubbing alcohol before sticking one down for the strongest hold.
