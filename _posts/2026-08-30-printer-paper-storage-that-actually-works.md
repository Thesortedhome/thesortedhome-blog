A printer takes up a strange amount of desk real estate for something used a few times a week, and paper multiplies faster than almost anything else in a home office — active documents, incoming mail, and old records you're not ready to shred all pile up differently and need different homes.

These four products cover the printer itself and the three separate paper problems it creates: active files that need to stay accessible, incoming mail that needs sorting, and records you need to keep but don't need on your desk.

## How to choose printer and paper storage that actually works

Sort paper by how often you touch it, not by what type of document it is — daily-use files belong in an accessible mobile cabinet, incoming mail needs a sorting spot that isn't the desk, and anything you only reference once or twice a year belongs in a closed box out of the way. Get the printer off the desk before tackling paper specifically; it's usually the single biggest object taking up prime desk space for the least frequent use. And resist the urge to buy one system for everything — active files, incoming paper, and archives genuinely need different containers because they're accessed at completely different frequencies.

## FAQ

**Where should a home printer actually go if not on the desk?**
A rolling printer stand beside or under the desk works best — it keeps the printer within reach for printing and stapling but frees the entire desktop for daily work.

**What's the best way to stop mail from piling up on the desk?**
A wall-mounted sorter with labeled tiers works better than a desktop tray for most people, since it's off the work surface entirely and makes the "what happens next" step visible at a glance.

**How long should I keep old paper files before shredding them?**
Tax documents are generally worth keeping for 3-7 years depending on your situation, and a portable, labeled file box by year makes it easy to find and clear out the oldest batch when it's time.
