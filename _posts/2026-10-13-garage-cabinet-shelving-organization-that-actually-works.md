Hooks and bins solve small-item storage, but most garages also need real capacity — a place for bulk paper towels, paint cans, power tools, or anything you don't want stacked directly on the floor. That's a different problem than wall hooks solve, and it usually needs actual shelving or a cabinet, not another organizer bin.

These three products cover that range: open shelving for bulk storage where visibility matters more than security, a lockable cabinet for anything you'd rather keep closed up, and a smaller wall cabinet for the in-between spot that's too small for a full shelving unit.

## 1. Heavy-duty shelving for bulk storage

For anything bought in bulk — paper towels, water cases, seasonal supplies — open shelving beats bins because you can see what's low at a glance. ## How to choose garage cabinets and shelving that actually work

Match the storage type to what's actually going in it: open shelving for anything you want visible and quick to grab, closed cabinets for anything you want out of sight or out of reach. Weight capacity matters more than most people budget for — a shelving unit rated for light-duty use will sag or fail under a genuine bulk-storage load, so check the per-shelf rating, not just the total. And measure your actual wall space before ordering; garage shelving units run wider and deeper than most people expect once they're standing next to a car.

## FAQ

**Should garage shelving be open or have doors?**
Depends on what's stored — open shelving is faster to use for anything grabbed often (paper goods, tools in rotation), while cabinets with doors are better for anything you want protected from dust, sunlight through a garage door window, or curious hands.

**How much weight can garage shelving actually hold?**
It varies a lot by unit, but a genuinely heavy-duty 5-tier shelf should be rated at 1,000+ lbs total, evenly distributed — always check the per-shelf rating specifically, since the total capacity assumes weight is spread evenly across every tier.

**Is a wall-mounted cabinet worth it if I already have floor shelving?**
Yes, if you have unused wall space — floor shelving and wall cabinets solve different problems, and a wall cabinet is often the only way to add closed storage to a garage that's already at floor capacity.
