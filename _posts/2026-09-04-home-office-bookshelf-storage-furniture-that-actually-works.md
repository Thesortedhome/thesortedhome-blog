These four pieces cover the storage a home office needs beyond the desk itself: vertical shelving for books and display items, a closed cabinet for things you'd rather not see, a modular cube system that adapts as your needs change, and bins that keep open shelves from turning into visual clutter.

## How to choose home office storage furniture that actually works

Decide what's meant to be seen and what isn't before buying furniture, not after — books and display items belong on open shelving, while cables, supplies, and anything visually messy belong behind closed doors or inside a bin. Favor modular or adjustable systems if your storage needs are still evolving; a fixed shelving unit that fits today's needs perfectly often stops fitting within a year. And use bins on open shelving as a middle ground — they let you keep something on visible shelving without it individually cluttering the view.

## FAQ

**Should I get open shelving or a closed cabinet for a home office?**
Both, generally — open shelving works for books and intentional display items, while a closed cabinet is better for anything you don't want visible, especially if the office is visible during video calls.

**Is modular cube storage sturdy enough for heavier items like books?**
Check the per-cube weight rating before loading it with books — most modular cube systems handle general supplies and lighter items well, but a dedicated bookshelf with a solid frame is usually the better choice for a genuinely heavy book collection.

**What's the best way to keep open shelving from looking cluttered?**
Storage bins are the simplest fix — grouping loose or mismatched items into a matching set of bins does more for the shelf's overall look than reorganizing the loose items themselves.
