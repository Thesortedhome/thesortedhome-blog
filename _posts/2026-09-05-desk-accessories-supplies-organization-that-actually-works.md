The stuff that clutters a desk surface every day usually isn't paperwork or cables — it's the small things used constantly and never put away: pens, a phone charger, sticky notes, a notebook. None of it makes sense boxed up in a drawer, but left loose on the desktop it's the first thing that makes an otherwise organized desk look messy.

## How to choose desk accessory organization that actually works

Sort by frequency, not category, when deciding what stays on the desktop versus what goes in a drawer — anything used multiple times a day earns a fixed spot on the surface, while true once-in-a-while items are better off in a drawer even if they're the "same kind" of object as something you use constantly. Keep the highest-frequency items closest to your dominant hand; the physical distance between where something lives and where you actually need it is what determines whether it gets put back or left out. And favor a small number of purpose-built holders over one catch-all tray — a single tray for "everything" tends to become a pile faster than a few specific holders do.

## FAQ

**What desk supplies should stay out versus go in a drawer?**
Anything used multiple times a day (pens, a notebook, a charger) earns a fixed spot on the desktop; anything used a few times a week or less is usually better off in a drawer where it's out of the way but still close.

**How do I stop charging cables from tangling on the desktop?**
A dedicated charging station with individual device slots solves this directly — it's a different fix from a cable clip or sleeve, which manage cord routing rather than giving devices themselves an upright, contained spot.

**What's the best way to organize occasional supplies like tape and paper clips?**
A rotating caddy works well for this specific category — supplies you need only occasionally still need to be findable in a few seconds, and a spinning base gets you there faster than digging through a flat drawer tray.
