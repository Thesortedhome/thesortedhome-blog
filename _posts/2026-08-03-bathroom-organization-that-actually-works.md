## How to choose bathroom storage that actually works

Humidity resistance matters more here than in almost any other room — before buying anything for a bathroom, check whether it's actually rated or reviewed for shower/humid use, not just "bathroom" in the product title. Favor mounted or wedged solutions (tension poles, pull-outs) over adhesive or suction-based ones, since moisture is exactly what makes adhesives and suction cups fail. And solve the counter and under-sink cabinet first — in a small bathroom, those two spots usually account for most of the visible daily clutter.

## FAQ

**Why do suction-cup shower organizers keep falling down?**
Humidity and soap residue both weaken suction over time, even on organizers rated for wet use — a tension-pole or wall-mounted caddy holds up far better long-term in an actual daily-use shower.

**What's the best way to organize a small bathroom with no linen closet?**
A woven basket or open shelving for towels, combined with a pull-out under-sink organizer for backstock — between the two, you cover both daily-use towels and bulk supplies without needing a dedicated closet.

**How do I stop bathroom counter clutter from coming back?**
Assign every item a specific tray or zone in a countertop organizer rather than a general "counter area" — clutter creeps back in fastest when there's unassigned flat space for things to land.
