## How to choose overhead garage storage that's actually safe

Weight rating is the one spec that matters most here, and the safety margin behind it matters even more — look for products tested well above their rated capacity (like the 750lb-rated, 1,200lb-tested rack above), not just rated at the number they advertise. Always mount into ceiling joists, never drywall or a finished ceiling surface alone; a stud finder rated for ceiling joists is worth the small cost before drilling anything overhead. And match the product to how often you need access — a fixed rack for things you store and forget, a hoist or sliding system for things you actually retrieve a few times a year.

## FAQ

**Is overhead garage storage actually safe for a two-car garage?**
Yes, when installed correctly into ceiling joists and kept within its rated weight limit — the real risk isn't the product itself, it's mounting into drywall alone or exceeding the rated capacity, both of which are avoidable with basic checks before installing.

**How much weight can a typical overhead garage rack hold?**
It varies significantly by product — smaller racks may hold a few hundred pounds, while heavy-duty 4x8 platforms like the one above can hold 750 lbs when properly installed. Always check the manufacturer's specific rating rather than assuming.

**What's the difference between an overhead rack and a ceiling hoist?**
A rack is fixed — good for things you store long-term and rarely touch. A hoist lowers and raises by hand — better for anything you need down and accessible several times a year, since you're not climbing a ladder each time.
